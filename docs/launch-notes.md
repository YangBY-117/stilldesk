# Launch narrative and visual provenance

The product story is the interruption it removes: an agent can work in the apps
you already use while your physical desktop remains yours. The release should
lead with one sentence, show a reproducible browser flow, and put setup directly
after the demo. Native-app coverage is shown with its actual limits.

The following public projects informed communication structure, not code:

- [agent-browser](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-browser): concise category
  statement, a small terminal example and concrete command documentation.
- [Browser Use](https://github.com/browser-use/browser-use): an immediately
  understandable job and a short path from installation to an example.
- [OpenClaw](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw): a memorable product identity,
  a direct action-oriented introduction and visible contribution paths.

StillDesk's distinguishing claim is background access without remote browser
debugging or a physical-input takeover. Do not transplant another project's
endorsements, adoption numbers, testimonials, performance claims or screenshots.
Do not fabricate stars, benchmarks or app support. Keep the versioned capability
matrix next to the promotional material.

## Short launch copy

> Your agent works. Your cursor stays yours.
>
> StillDesk gives agents background interfaces to Chrome, Edge and native apps
> through MCP and a CLI. Browser control uses a normal extension and Native
> Messaging, without remote debugging. You keep using your desktop.
>
> Start with the verified browser workflows, then add account-scoped app
> adapters. Every provider states what works and what is still unavailable.
